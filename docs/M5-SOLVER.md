## 1. The m4 error table does not exist yet

The brief asks what each method "fixes in the m4 error table". At the time of writing there is no such table: crash-sim `validation/` holds only the reference pulses, their meta files and the converter (`validation/test-a.pulse.csv`, `test-a.meta`, `test-b.pulse.csv`, `test-b.meta`, `convert.py`, `README.md`); there is no `validation/CALIBRATION.md`, no pulse report in `src/main.rs` (no `pulse_report` text in it) and no `scenarios/validation_*.bsn` `[read: directory listing, 0bb2b5e]`. `src/pulse.rs` has the filter and the metrics (`cfc_filter` :86, `delta_v` :145, `peak` :153, `duration` :171, `olc` :205) but nothing runs a car against a pulse yet.

So the "fixes" column below is a **hypothesis from the model's structure**, tied to the six gated rows of the pulse report (`dv_mps`, `peak_mps2`, `peak_time_s`, `duration_s`, `olc_mps2`, `static_crush_m`; `docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:79-90`). Section 7 lists the readings of the real table that would confirm or kill the recommendation, and the gate that makes n20-11's calibration wait for them.

## 2. What the node-beam solver is, and where it is thin (all `[read]`)

- **The trait.** `Solver::step(&mut self, State, h)` and `step_with_forces(&mut self, State, extra, h)` (`src/solver.rs:23-39`). `State` carries `pos`, `vel`, `beams`, `mass`, `gravity` and nothing else (`:11-19`). The doc says a solver "may keep its own working data between calls but must not need anything else from the caller" (`:21-22`). The default `step_with_forces` is operator splitting (`:32-38`), so any solver takes the wheels' forces.
- **The default.** `Sim::new` and `Sim::from_body` use `SymplecticEuler` (`src/lib.rs:325`, `:341`); `Sim::from_body_with_solver` (`:356`) and `Sim::from_car_with_solver` (`:376`) take any `Box<dyn Solver>`; the field is `solver: Box<dyn Solver>` (`:279`). `tests/solver.rs:71` (`a_second_solver_plugs_in`) already runs a second solver through `Sim::with_solver`, and `:12` (`default_csv_matches_main_fixture`) pins the default bytes.
- **The step.** Beam force loop then `vel += force * h / m`, `pos += vel * h` (`src/solver.rs:60-86`). Everything else, in the order of `Sim::integrate` (`src/lib.rs:514-551`): the solver, the wheels (`Car::pre_step`, `post_step`), then `collide::apply` for the ground and walls (`:525`, `:550`), then breaks and the yield pass (`substep`, `:628-634`).
- **Plasticity** is a rest-length move per beam, after the substep. Tensile yield is unchanged: `rest' = len / (1 + y)`. Compressive yield alone changed: a beam is crushed past yield when `(len - rest) / rest0 < -(y + YIELD_EPS)` and gets `rest' = len + y*rest0`, so the force stays `k*y*rest0` (`rest0` = the beam's original rest length) (`src/lib.rs:613-654`). Nothing stops `rest` shrinking while the beam keeps being squeezed; its only bound is that it stays positive and finite (`:644`).
- **Contact** is node against infinite plane with `RESTITUTION` 0.5 above `REST_SPEED` 0.2 m/s (`src/lib.rs:42`, `:47`; `src/collide.rs:74-98`), applied by `Sim` after every solver, so a new solver does not change it (`src/collide.rs:6-7`). There is no node-to-node or beam-to-beam contact anywhere in `collide.rs`.
- **Limits that abort a run.** `Config::validate_body` and `build_from_body` refuse a step above the Gershgorin bound (`src/lib.rs:207-215`, `:406-408`; `src/solver.rs:128`, `:153-191`); `Sim::check` aborts on a non-finite state or a beam strain over `MAX_STRAIN_LIMIT` 0.5 (`:53`, `:674-709`). Both only know about beams.
- **Cost.** One substep of the 40-node, 240-beam car costs 0.005 ms in all, of which the solver step is 0.001612 ms, about 6.7 ns per beam (`docs/PERF_HOTSPOTS.md:52`, `:59`; 40 nodes, 240 beams: `scenarios/pad_and_wall.bsn`, `grep -c "^beam"` = 240, `^node` = 40). That is single-threaded wall time (`docs/PERF_HOTSPOTS.md:121`).

**Where it is thin for a crash pulse (hypotheses, section 1).**

- **H1, no bottoming-out.** A squeezed plastic beam keeps shortening at the yield force; nothing makes the crush zone stiffen when its parts meet (engine, firewall). Expected effect: crush too deep and no late rise in the pulse, so `static_crush_m`, `duration_s` and `olc_mps2` miss. This is the only gap I can name from the code alone.
- **H2, a stiffness ceiling from the step.** Explicit stability bounds `k` (`h^2 w2 + 2 h g < 4`, `src/solver.rs:183`). With the m4 range `crush.area_m2` up to 0.002 m^2 and E = 210 GPa (`docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:171-175`), `k = E A / L_c`. `[derived; car_basic numbers assumed for the unknown validation car: L_c 0.75 m, lightest node 14 kg, 17 beams at the busiest node, from `docs/FORMAT.md:73`]`: at A = 0.002, k = 5.6e8 N/m, w2 = 2 x 17 x 5.6e8 / 14 = 1.4e9 /s^2, so the limit is about 54 microseconds, under the frozen 0.1 ms; the limit reaches 0.1 ms near A = 6e-4. That would refuse the top three of the seven area values, 60 of the 140 grid points (`docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:185`: "A point refused for its step limit is logged as `refused`"). The real count is in the m4 table.
- **H3, wall contact is a fixed constant.** The first nodes to reach the wall lose three quarters of their speed at once (restitution 0.5). That is outside any solver (above) and outside the calibrated quantities (`docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:179`, "a finding for m5 or `IDEAS.md`, never a new knob"). It is the strongest reason the recommendation could be wrong (section 7).

## 3. The three methods compared

"n20-05" = sparse matrices and CG. Costs are for the 0.2 s run of the run_flags frozen line (`docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:150-154`) on this PC, **single thread**: no step of any option here is long enough to pay for thread hand-offs (a step is 20 to 500 microseconds), and `numerics` and crash-sim are both single-threaded today (`projects/numerics/README.md:7`). The 16 cores are therefore **not** assumed. `rtf` = wall time of the solver loop / sim time, gated at 10 (`docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:150`, `:296`).

### The numbers behind the table

**(1) Implicit.** `[derived]` Scalar non-zeros of `K_t` = 9 x (nodes + 2 x beams) (one 3 x 3 block per node on the diagonal, two per beam): 4 680 for 40 nodes and 240 beams, 37 440 for 320 nodes and 1 920 beams. `[assumed]` one SpMV costs about 1 ns per non-zero (memory bound) and a CG iteration about 1.3 SpMVs, so 6 microseconds and 49 microseconds. `[derived]` the system matrix `M/h^2 + K` has condition number about `1 + h^2 w2_max` (the lowest mode of a free body is zero), 25 to 1 600 for h = 0.5 ms and `w2_max` between 1e8 and 6.4e9; CG to 1e-8 needs about `0.5 sqrt(kappa) ln(2e8)` = 50 to 380 iterations; Jacobi may help a little (the figure is a ceiling). 400 steps of 0.5 ms, 3 Newton solves each: 60 000 to 480 000 iterations: 0.36 to 2.9 s (car_basic size) or 2.9 to 24 s (8 times larger) for 0.2 s of sim. Explicit node-beam for comparison `[derived]` 2 000 substeps x 6.7 ns x beams = 3.2 ms (car_basic size) or 26 ms (8 times larger).

**(2) Tets.** `[assumed]` Zone: at most 300 tets (about 50 hexahedral cells of 0.2 m, each cut into 6 tets). Per tet per substep about 800 flops (deformation gradient, its inverse and the log of its determinant for the Neo-Hookean stress, a per-step stiffness bound, nodal forces; a return mapping with a 3 x 3 eigen step only when the tet yields) at about 2 Gflop/s effective, so 0.4 microsecond; plus the beams outside (about 1 900 beams x 6.7 ns = 13 microseconds) and wheels and contact (about 10 microseconds, `docs/PERF_HOTSPOTS.md:49-57` scale). Per substep 300 x 0.4 + 23 = 143 microseconds. 0.2 s at h = 20 microseconds is 10 000 substeps: 1.4 s, rtf 7.2. At 0.25 microsecond per tet rtf is 4.9; at 0.6 it is 10.2, so a measured per-tet cost over 0.6 microsecond breaks the cap at 300 tets and the tet count must come down (R15 stops the first calibration if rtf is over the cap).

*Step limit* `[derived]` for the worst corner of the proposed ranges: `E = sigma_p / eps_y = 2.5e6 / 0.01 = 2.5e8` Pa, Poisson ratio 0.3, so the dilatational modulus at rest is `E (1 - nu) / ((1 + nu)(1 - 2 nu))` = 3.4e8 Pa; the zone density of a 1 330 kg car's front is `[assumed]` 150 to 300 kg/m^3, so `c_d` = 1 060 to 1 500 m/s; the smallest altitude of a tet of 0.2 m cells cut along the cell diagonal is 0.141 m; `altitude / c_d` = 94 to 133 microseconds; a safety factor of 0.5 for the tet's shape and the lumped-mass bound gives 47 to 66 microseconds at rest. **The Neo-Hookean tangent is not constant**: it stiffens as a tet is compressed (terms in `F^-1`), and it also changes under stretch, so the rest-state number is not a bound for the run. The law caps the compressive stress while a tet is yielding but not after the lock-up strain, and never caps tension. So the bound is enforced **per substep from the current deformation gradient** (R5), and the planning number lets the stiffness double (frequency times 1.41): 33 to 47 microseconds. N5 = 50 is h = 20 microseconds, 1.65 to 2.35 times inside it. The code's own bounds replace this estimate, and n20-11 stops before calibration if any grid corner is over it.

**(3) Shells.** `[derived]` explicit step L / c with `c = sqrt(E / (rho (1 - nu^2)))` = 5.4 km/s. Whether a fold forms needs about 4 elements per fold half-wave; fold half-waves of body sheet are tens of mm and industry meshes are 5 to 10 mm `[memory: M2]`. A crush zone of about 6 m^2 of sheet at 5 mm is 240 000 elements; at 0.4 microsecond per element-substep and 200 000 substeps (1 microsecond) that is 19 000 s for 0.2 s of sim, rtf 1e5. Even a perfect 16-core split is rtf 6 000. 100 mm shells are 600 elements and rtf about 13 but cannot fold, so they offer no fidelity over node-beam.

## 4. Recommendation: (2), explicit linear tetrahedra in the crush zone

**Choose (2) with these three decisions.**

1. **Explicit, one integrator for all nodes.** The tets and the beams add into one force array and one symplectic Euler update (the pattern of `src/solver.rs:60-86`). No linear algebra: **n20-05 is not needed.**
2. **Hyperelastic elasticity with an energy barrier, plasticity of crushable-foam type.** Compressible Neo-Hookean elasticity, `W = mu/2 (I1 - 3) - mu ln J + lambda/2 (ln J)^2`: the forces are the exact gradient of an energy (so the energy test is a real test, section 5) and the energy goes to infinity as the volume `J` goes to zero, so a finite force cannot squash a tet flat or turn it inside out. (Saint Venant-Kirchhoff, which an earlier version of this note used, has finite energy at `J = 0` and its force falls toward zero under strong compression, so it does not bottom out; it is rejected for that reason.) Plus a compressive plastic flow with a lock-up limit (section 8, R4). Not J2 plasticity: J2 flow is incompressible, so a 60% crush would make the zone bulge sideways instead of compacting, which is the opposite of what the structure does (it is mostly air). Not co-rotational elasticity: forms that are the gradient of an energy exist, but they need the derivative of the polar-decomposition rotation, which costs more per tet, and the linear co-rotational stiffness stays finite when a tet collapses, so it has the same missing barrier as Saint Venant-Kirchhoff; the common cheaper form (rotation held fixed in the force) is not an energy gradient, so it can create energy under rotation and would make the energy test meaningless.
3. **A conforming mesh: tets and beams share nodes.** No interpolation, no constraint, no mass transfer (section 5).

**Why not (1).** It cannot beat node-beam on the physics: same law, same contact. Its only possible win is numerical, either by moving the calibration to stiff values the explicit step refused (H2) or by its damping (which the noise rule `S_B(m5) + d(m5) < S_B(node-beam) - d(node-beam)` penalises). It also costs more than it saves at car size (rtf 15 to 118 for an 8-times-larger car, above). **Why not (3).** It fails the run-time cap by 1 to 4 orders of magnitude at a mesh that can fold, and at a mesh that fits it cannot fold. It also needs a surface mesh, a shell record and rotational dofs the format and `State` do not have.

**What (2) costs.** A format change (section 8, n20-06a), a trait with two defaulted methods, about two coding tasks, and a run time within a factor 2 of the cap on estimates that nobody has measured.

**Fallback, not an m5 option.** A node-beam with a floor on plastic shortening would address H1 in about 20 lines of `apply_yield`. I do not recommend it as m5: Stage 6 item 5 says "soft bodies or FEM in the crash zone" (`docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:6`), and a changed node-beam is not a different solver (it would also change the baseline the m5 verdict compares against, never re-run, `docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:293`). It is listed so that Lazlo can choose it knowingly if section 7's readings say H1 is the whole story.

## 5. Coupling at the crush-zone boundary, and the test

**Conforming, shared nodes.** A node is in the boundary if it belongs to at least one tet and at least one beam. There is one `pos`, one `vel`, one `mass` per node (`State`, `src/solver.rs:11-19`). A solver cannot add mass, so the tets add none: the node masses in the file stay the lumped masses, and the car's total mass is unchanged (setup rule 1 of the pulse report, `docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:92`).

- **Positions pass** by identity: a tet and a beam on one node read the same `pos[i]`.
- **Forces pass** by summation: in each substep the beam forces are added first (index order), then the tet forces (index order), into the same per-node array, then one `vel += force * h / m`, `pos += vel * h`. The force on a shared node is the sum of the beam part and the tet part; neither side overwrites the other.
- **Order is fixed** (beams, then tets, then the extra forces if any), so the bits are fixed.
- **A non-conforming join is out of scope**: a tet face against a beam mid-span has no node to share. The `tet` record (n20-06a) refuses nothing about it (the format cannot know); the validation files are built conforming by construction (R12).
- **Plasticity** lives inside the tets (plastic strain per tet) and the beams outside are elastic (the `crush` beams are replaced by tets, section 8 R12), so no plastic state crosses the boundary.

**Why energy cannot be created there.** Beams and Neo-Hookean tets are each the exact gradient of an energy, so the sum is, whatever the nodes they share. The one integrator is semi-implicit Euler. `[derived]` For a linear system `K`, `M`, step `v' = v - h M^-1 K x`, `x' = x + h v'`, the quantity `H = 1/2 v^T M v + 1/2 x^T K x - (h/2) v^T K x`, built from the start-of-step `v` and `x`, is **exactly unchanged by one step** (checked by expanding the three terms of `H` at `(v', x')` for one degree of freedom: the coefficients of `v^2`, `x^2` and `vx` match those at `(v, x)`). Since `|v^T K x| <= (v^T K v)^(1/2) (x^T K x)^(1/2) <= omega_max (2 KE)^(1/2) (2 PE)^(1/2) <= omega_max E`, the true energy `E` stays within

`E_k <= E_0 (1 + q) / (1 - q)`,   `q = h omega_max / 2`,

with `omega_max` the largest circular frequency (the code's Gershgorin value). With `h omega_max <= 0.2` the bound is 1.22. A broken coupling (a boundary force counted twice, dropped, or with the wrong sign) makes the energy drift past it, because it breaks the gradient structure. A boundary node's mass counted twice is different: the energy bound still passes, and only the momentum check catches it, failing at substep 1. This was measured in n20-11 core (t-20261121-021841), test `m5_coupling_creates_no_energy`; the energy bound alone does not catch it.

**The test, `m5_coupling_creates_no_energy` (named in `docs/STAGE6-M4-M5.md:303`).**

- Body: a block of tets (2 x 2 x 2 cells, 48 tets) and a block of beams (2 cells, all node pairs as in `scenarios/car_basic.bsn`, `docs/FORMAT.md:73`), joined at one shared face of 9 nodes. Tet solid never yields (plateau stress 1e12 Pa) and has damping 0; the beams have damping 0. Gravity 0, body placed 100 m above the ground so no plane touches it, no wheels.
- Start: a uniform shear-and-stretch strain of 1e-3 plus a rigid velocity and spin, so energy sits in both blocks and a wave crosses the shared face.
- Step `h = limit / 10` where `limit = 2 / omega_max` is the solver's own value; 20 000 substeps.
- Assert at every substep: `E_k <= E_0 (1 + q)/(1 - q) (1 + 1e-2)` with `E = KE + beam energy + tet energy`; the `1e-2` is the named allowance for the nonlinear (Neo-Hookean) terms at strain 1e-3 (the derivation above is exact for a linear system only; at strain 1e-3 the tangent differs from the rest tangent by a relative 1e-3, taken 10 times; the test does not claim an exact shadow energy for the nonlinear law, only this bound at that strain). Also linear momentum and angular momentum conserved to 1e-9 of their scale (a tet force from a rotation-invariant energy has zero net force and torque; `[derived]` `L' - L = h sum_i (x_i cross f_i) = 0`).
- **Prove it can fail** (playbook 10): in the coder's worktree, flip the sign of the tet force on the shared nodes only, run, show the test fails; restore; `git diff` shows only the intended change. Second mutation: count the shared-node mass twice. Both are reported in the n20-11 evidence.

## 6. Does n20-05 belong there, with numerics already on main?

**No: drop n20-05 as written, and do not board anything in its place for this recommendation.**

- What n20-05 would build already exists in numerics `main`: `Csr` (`projects/numerics/src/sparse.rs:30`), `Csr::from_triplets` summing duplicates in (row, column, input) order (`:55`), `mul_vec` (`:159`), `diag` (`:175`), and `pcg`, Jacobi-preconditioned, deterministic, refusing non-square, size mismatch and a non-positive diagonal, reporting non-convergence as an error and returning the true residual (`:241`; doc `:1-26`). Its own header says it was compared with the n20-05 brief (`:16-26`) and lists the differences (`pcg` not `cg`, optional start vector, its own error enum). A second copy inside crash-sim (`src/numerics.rs`, the n20-05 claim) would duplicate a reviewed module.
- Under (2) nothing needs it. Under (1), the remaining task would not be "write CSR and CG" but "consume numerics from crash-sim", and that has an open problem: crash-sim's `Cargo.toml` has an empty `[dependencies]` and its own `[workspace]` (`Cargo.toml:6-9`), and a relative path dependency `../numerics` resolves against the manifest's folder, which is `projects/crash-sim` on main but `worktrees/crash-sim/<task id>` in every task worktree, so it would not resolve there. ADR 0003 (dependency policy) is Proposed, not accepted (`docs/adr/0003-dependency-policy.md:3`), and ADR 0004 names "a numerics core needed by two projects" as the ADR 0001 trigger T4 (`docs/adr/0004-roadmap-repo-map.md:177`, `:184`). That is Lazlo's decision (section 9), not a coder's.
- If Lazlo later wants (1) anyway: the caller must keep `K_t` positive semi-definite (`pcg` returns `Domain` when `p^T A p <= 0`, `projects/numerics/src/sparse.rs:347`), which for beams means dropping the negative geometric term of a compressed beam.

Other things that would make it wrong, each with how it would show:

- **The per-tet cost is above 0.6 microsecond** (`rtf` over 10 at 300 tets, `m5_rtf_max`): shown by the first n20-11 timing. The lever is fewer tets, which is a coarser zone and fewer degrees of freedom than node-beam's lattice, so the advantage fades. R15 stops the first calibration run on it.
- **The compaction law is unstable** under 60 to 70% uniaxial crush (a tet reaches the inverted-tet rule, or the per-step stiffness bound is exceeded): shown by R4's and R5's tests. The Neo-Hookean energy barrier means no finite force reaches `J = 0`, but an explicit step can still jump across it, which is why the inverted-tet rule fails closed (R4e) instead of continuing; it is the least proven part of this note. Fallback inside (2): a smaller `h` (N5 raised before calibration) or a compressive stiffening cap, said so in `docs/PHYSICS.md`.
